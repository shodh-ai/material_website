Registered Fourier-phase geometry after bfloat16 storage:
separating reconstruction from learned feature quality

\section*{Abstract}

Accurate reconstruction from a hybrid representation need not demonstrate the quality of
its learned features. We examine a captured organic-crystal representation containing 32 carrier
channels, 30 deterministic signed Fourier-phase channels and 130 neural residual channels per
atom. Source dependency analysis establishes that fractional-coordinate reconstruction uses
only the phase channels: the frozen decoder discards its batch argument and does not use the
residual channels. An isolated NumPy implementation independently decoded all 1200 stored
base-state payloads, covering 134 parent identifiers, with 9600 checks passing. Reference-cell
coordinate root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) had median 1.68188 [U+0002] 10\ensuremath{-}4 and maximum
3.09576 [U+0002] 10\ensuremath{-}4 Angstrom; the maximum discrepancy from saved RMSD values was 3.16 [U+0002] 10\ensuremath{-}8
Angstrom. Cell reconstruction was less precise, with maximum length and angle errors of
0.07565 Angstrom and 0.131842 degrees. Saved transformation results support registered origin,
proper-rotation and permutation actions, but not general cell-basis equivalence. We retain an
original failed origin test and explain its subsequent measurement-adapter correction. These
results establish stored geometric recoverability and narrowly specified covariance, not learned
semantic quality, compression superiority or predictive utility.

\section*{1 Introduction and contributions}

Crystal representations must reconcile geometric information with equivalences induced by coordi-
nate conventions. A rigid rotation changes Cartesian coordinates without changing the material;
an origin shift changes fractional coordinates; atom permutations change row order. Whether a
representation should remain identical, transform covariantly or require registration depends on
its intended use. These alternatives are scientifically different, even when each produces a high
similarity score after an appropriate comparison.
A second distinction concerns hybrid representations that combine deterministic geometry with
neural outputs. If coordinates are explicitly encoded in some channels, successful reconstruction
can establish the fidelity of those channels and their numerical readout without testing whether the
neural features capture useful chemistry. The relevant question is therefore not simply whether a
decoder reconstructs a crystal, but which stored information actually determines its output.
We address that question for a frozen, 192-channel-per-atom organic-crystal representation stored
in bfloat16 (BF16). The focused contributions are a source-level dependency analysis of its coor-
dinate readout, an independent reconstruction of every captured base-state payload, and an inter-
pretation of registered symmetry tests that preserves both a historical failure and its later adapter
correction. Rotation-invariant cell Gram information provides the geometric context, while sep-
arate cell errors prevent the exceptionally small fractional-coordinate readout error from being
mistaken for equally precise recovery of the full physical cell. The argument concerns this captured
representation and cohort, rather than a general claim of unique crystal encoding.

\section*{2 Related work}

SLICES studies an invertible, invariant crystal string representation for inverse design using gener-
ative deep learning [ 1]. Mat2Seq studies invariant crystalline sequences for language-model-enabled
generation [ 2]. These approaches contextualize the importance of invertibility and invariance, but
their objectives should not be conflated with registered comparison of a continuous hybrid repre-
sentation.
No matched SLICES or Mat2Seq benchmark is supplied for the present cohort. Accordingly,
published aggregate scores are not compared with the errors reported here, and no common recon-
struction metric or compression advantage is assumed. The signed-phase and group-action algebra
used below is established mathematics, not a priority claim. Our contribution is the evidence-
based separation of what this particular stored reconstruction measures from what would require
evaluation of learned features.

\section*{3 Scientific methods and validation protocols}


\section*{3.1 Cohort and evidential scope}

This study is a retrospective reanalysis of captured arrays, serialized payloads, reports and source
code; no additional simulations or learned-model inference were performed. The frozen cohort
contains 1200 unique frames, with 400 each labelled val\_sel, val\_rep and ood. Parent identifiers
parsed from the documented sample-identifier convention number 45, 45 and 44, respectively, with
no overlap between roles. Thus the cohort covers 134 parent identifiers, not 1200 independent
materials. Atom counts range from 14 to 252, with median 136.
The loader receipt describes a larger 8000-frame, 585-parent corpus, including 6800 training
frames, parent-split disjointness and unique frame hashes. Those larger claims were not indepen-
dently rebuilt from all parent arrays. In particular, the present checks do not independently repro-
duce training-parent exclusion. Selection-labelled validation data, val\_sel, are not an untouched
independent test; the label ood alone does not establish distributional novelty or transferability. All
reported quantiles describe pooled frames without iid confidence intervals or inferential significance.

\section*{3.2 Channel structure and decoder dependencies}

Each atom stores 32 carrier channels, 30 deterministic phase channels and 130 neural residual
channels. Zero-based full-token slices are 0:32, 32:62 and 62:192, respectively. Although the adapter
historically names the entire 32:192 slice as learned, only its 130-channel residual is neural output.
The encoder explicitly concatenates deterministic absolute phases with the retained neural-head

\section*{3.5 Independent checks and saved transformation tests}

An isolated, credential-free recipe using Python 3.12.14 and NumPy 2.3.5 parsed all 1200 original
base-state envelopes and payloads. It reconstructed float32 values from BF16 bit patterns, decoded
the 30 phase channels, checked retained ordered atomic numbers and common cell rows, and recom-
puted coordinate and cell errors. Supplied Python modules were not executed, and original Torch
checkpoint objects were not deserialized. The coordinate-metric comparison tolerance, declared be-
fore examining full-cohort results, was 10\ensuremath{-}6 Angstrom: NumPy and original CUDA float32 atan2
implementations need not be bit-identical.
Transformation metrics instead remain saved-report/source evidence because transformed pay-
loads were not supplied for this independent decode. The saved registered-cosine threshold is
> 0.9999, with decoded origin and rotation covariance RMSD required to be below 0.01 Angstrom.
Base reconstruction limits were 0.15 Angstrom for coordinate RMSD, 0.25 Angstrom for length
error, one degree for angle error and 0.05 relative-volume error. These are diagnostic tolerances,
not physical-property criteria. Cell sensitivity compares only the 130 residual channels under three
prescribed perturbations: 5\% isotropic scaling, diagonal stretch (1.08, 0.96, 1) and an off-diagonal
shear of 0.08. These perturbations retain fractional coordinates and atomic identities and left-
multiply the row-basis cell; the shear entry is in the first row and second column. The diagonal
stretch therefore scales lattice rows, not necessarily Cartesian principal axes. Its measurable-change
threshold is residual RMS > 10\ensuremath{-}5.

\section*{4.2 Original origin failure and corrected registration}

The original 1200-case report had zero passing origin rows and minimum nominal-noncarrier co-
sine 0.4894573469. A subsequently frozen forensic protocol compared the historical inverse-phase
helper, analytic controls and adapter behavior across the same full cohort. It identified an omitted
registration override: the old adapter inherited an identity operation despite declaring a registered
phase action.
Adding only that override routed registration through the existing inverse-phase helper; the
source abstract syntax tree was otherwise unchanged. Saved forensic rows contain 1200 historical-
helper passes, 1200 analytic-control passes and 1200 corrected passes, with no repack, correspon-
dence or nonfinite failures. The saved classification is HARNESS\_IMPLEMENTATION\_MISMATCH. A new
isolated 28-check recipe independently verified source, identity and aggregate relationships without
rerunning neural inference. The original failure, protocol, all forensic rows and correction evidence

\section*{5 Discussion and limitations}

The central result is a separation of three evidential levels. First, source dependencies identify
which information determines fractional reconstruction. Second, independent numerical decoding
demonstrates that the specified mechanism works on every stored base payload. Third, saved
transformed-input measurements characterize registered actions, with independent checks restricted
to their recorded arithmetic and source relationships. Keeping these levels distinct avoids describing
a stored-data reconstruction as an independently reproduced learned encoder.
The coexistence of recoverable signed phases and rotation-invariant Gram information is useful
conceptually. Origin-dependent coordinate information need not be erased to permit meaningful
comparison: it can instead carry an explicit group action. Conversely, rotation invariance of a
cell descriptor does not provide invariance to general integer basis changes. Proper rotations were
tested, but these results do not establish reflection or handedness handling, arbitrary-origin byte
invariance, full cell-basis invariance or universal crystal uniqueness. The representation comparison
also excludes carrier channels, so a high noncarrier cosine says nothing about equality of complete
payload bytes.
Residual sensitivity is a narrower result than learned utility. Nonconstant responses to cell
changes can rule out collapse under those perturbations without establishing useful response mag-
nitude, chemically meaningful organization or accurate predictions. Similarly, retained atomic
numbers cannot validate the separate learned species head. Geometry recovery alone establishes
neither energy/force accuracy, material stability nor downstream predictive performance.

The cohort boundaries matter even for such narrowly framed conclusions. Multiple frames share
parent identifiers; selection-labelled validation contributes to pooled quantiles; and source-labelled
out-of-distribution data do not independently demonstrate transfer. The latest 1200-case analysis
is kept separate from older 300-case reconstruction and other crystal-projection cohorts because
their decoder and cohort relationships have not been reconciled. The present evidence supports a
focused stored-geometry result, rather than a broad materials-model benchmark.

\section*{6 Conclusion}

All 1200 captured BF16 base payloads support independent geometric decoding through deter-
ministic signed Fourier phases, with coordinate errors near 10\ensuremath{-}4 Angstrom under a reference-cell
metric. Source analysis establishes why this precision cannot be credited to the 130 neural residual
channels. Separate cell errors and explicitly registered transformation comparisons define the actual
scope of recovery. Preserving the original failed origin test alongside its adapter-only correction
further shows that a symmetry result depends on implementing the declared action. Stored geo-
metric fidelity, registered covariance and learned feature quality are distinct scientific claims and
require distinct evidence.
