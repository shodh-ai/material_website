Reconstruction Accuracy and Irreversible Ordering in Quantized
Cyclic-Mechanics Fields

\section*{Abstract}

Small field-reconstruction error does not necessarily preserve irreversible state evolution. We
examine this distinction in a saved reconstruction experiment using a reduced, dimensionless
central-force lattice family and an inherited strict-bottleneck GridConvAE. Native 323 solutions
were exported as eleven 17-channel 643 frames by interpolation; material names denote normal-
ized property labels rather than independently calibrated materials. The principal comparison
decodes the same BF16 token representation on the same 512 separately seeded holdout trajec-
tories, before and after a decoded-only damage/plastic constraint. Raw pooled stress and kine-
matics normalized root-mean-square errors (nRMSE) were 0.0554055721 and 0.0589356678, but
the raw decoder produced 31,605,800 damage and 639,173,958 accumulated-plastic-strain decre-
ments, each against 1,342,177,280 adjacent voxel comparisons. Clipping followed by cumulative
maxima eliminated these decrements by construction, while damage/plastic nRMSE changed
from 0.0015730692/0.0567105503 to 0.0014920677/0.0532703537. Damage error is materially
dependent on its frozen denominator floor. Transition-frame reconstruction outperformed copy-
ing baselines, and a separate saved transport audit reported exact bit round trips. These results
support reconstruction through the declared quantized interface, not learned irreversibility, con-
stitutive validation, forecasting, or fatigue-life prediction.

\section*{1 Introduction and contributions}

Scientific field compression must preserve more than small average discrepancies when downstream
interpretation depends on temporal inequalities. In cyclic mechanics, damage and accumulated
equivalent plastic strain are state variables whose decreases can contradict the intended irreversible
description. A reconstruction can remain close to its reference in a pooled norm while repeatedly
reversing these variables. Quantization and transport introduce another distinction: accuracy
measured directly from a floating-point latent need not describe the representation actually stored
and decoded.
The question addressed here is therefore specific: does small reconstruction error from BF16 token
values establish irreversible ordering, and what changes when a decoded-only ordering constraint
is added? We answer using a controlled comparison on the same 512-trajectory holdout from a
saved experiment. Both paths share the trained checkpoint, supplied frames, normalization, token
quantization, and learned decoder. The only intervention is deterministic post-processing of damage
and accumulated plastic strain.
The empirical contribution has three parts. First, generator qualification makes the scope and
remaining modal concentration of the source fields explicit. Second, paired raw-versus-contracted
decoding separates magnitude accuracy from temporal admissibility without comparing different
campaigns as though they were an ablation. Third, copying baselines and serialization checks
test whether the constrained result is merely an ordered but uninformative output or an artifact

of bypassing the quantized interface. Neither the lattice simulator, GridConvAE, nor cumulative
maximum is proposed as a new architecture or constitutive law.

\section*{2 Related work}

Scientific compression literature distinguishes errors in primary fields from errors in quantities
derived from them. A hybrid autoencoder pipeline combines quantization, residual processing,
and constrained post-processing to control primary-data and quantity-of-interest errors [ 1]. This
provides relevant context for separating reconstruction from constraint satisfaction. Here, however,
the final ordering operation receives decoded values only, not true quantities of interest, and it
supplies no general primary-field error bound.
Fixed-rate floating-point array compression provides a complementary context: block transforms
and embedded coding support specified storage rates and localized access [ 2]. Those objectives differ
from testing temporal ordering in a learned bottleneck. Neither reference supplies a measured
baseline on the present lattice data. No comparative rate–distortion or runtime advantage over
established scientific codecs is established by this study.

\section*{3 Scientific methods}


\section*{3.1 Reduced lattice family and exported fields}

The source generator is a NumPy implementation of a damped, nonperiodic central-force lattice.
Thirteen undirected neighbor families include axial, face-diagonal, and body-diagonal bonds. For
endpoints i, j, normalized stiffnesses Ei, Ej, and lattice bond length Lij, the code assigns stiffnessp
EiEj/L2
ij and the lower endpoint yield value. Trial extension subtracts signed plastic extension;
excess trial force above a hardening-dependent cap updates signed plasticity and accumulated
equivalent plastic strain. Bond damage combines cyclic overstress and plastic-work contributions
and reduces subsequent bond forces. Equal and opposite endpoint forces are scattered to the lattice,
with symmetric nodal stress accumulation.
Geometry varies through dog-bone neck dimensions and offset, ellipticity, a surface notch, inclu-
sions, and smooth Fourier material disorder. Aluminum, titanium, steel, and composite are labels
selecting normalized density, stiffness, and yield parameters. Retained dimensional constants doc-
ument provenance but do not calibrate the dimensionless solve to these materials. One end is
fixed; the other receives uniform axial traction and two nonuniform transverse traction patterns.
With the specimen axis along x, the pattern named bending applies y-directed force proportional
to centered z, generating torque about x rather than a transverse bending moment. The pattern
named torsion applies transverse forces proportional to negative centered z and centered y, respec-
tively. Four named load categories select dominant pattern amplitudes while retaining secondary
phases and frequency ratios. The nonuniform patterns are RMS-normalized to control their nominal
amplitudes; their names do not establish independent bending and torsional loading modes.
Velocity advances using internal, external, and damping forces; displacement uses the average of
old and new velocities. The documented configuration uses eight substeps per exported interval
and eleven frames. Native fields are solved on 323 grids and exported to 643: continuous channels
use trilinear interpolation, while support, relative density, and material labels use nearest-neighbor
export. The export is not native 643 physical resolution or a convergence demonstration.

The 17 channels comprise relative density, three velocities, damage, accumulated plastic strain,
von Mises stress, material identifier, three displacements, and six stresses. Despite their names,
ux, uy, and uz are velocity channels. Exported velocities are rederived by temporal differentiation
of displacement; von Mises stress is recomputed from stress components; damage and plastic his-
tories are made monotone. Consequently, successful checks of these identities partly test declared
construction operations, not independent physical predictions.

\section*{3.2 Frame codec and quantized representation}

The inherited no-skip GridConvAE receives one normalized 17-channel frame and a plane containing
normalized time t/10. Its configuration uses base width 48, two residual blocks per stage, a 64\ensuremath{\times}163
latent, GroupNorm, SiLU activations, strided 3D convolutions, and nearest-neighbor upsampling
followed by convolutions. Internal residual blocks do not bypass the encoder–decoder bottleneck.
Exact reshaping of 23 latent patches yields 512 tokens of 512 coefficients per frame. Eleven supplied
frames yield 5,632 token rows. The reported model has 8.233 million parameters.
The nominal 17\ensuremath{\times} reduction compares 4,456,448 input-field coefficients with 262,144 latent coef-
ficients per frame. It is a coefficient-count reduction, not a measured compressed-filesystem ratio:
model weights, metadata, precision differences, and archive overhead are not included. The encoder
observes each supplied current frame; the normalized-time plane does not turn this reconstruction
task into future-state prediction.
Tokens are quantized to native BF16 and represented as uint16 bit patterns, rather than nu-
merically converted to unsigned integers. The evaluator restores these patterns as BF16, converts
the restored token values to float32 for decoding, and reverses channel normalization. A sepa-
rate saved transport audit serialized C-order bytes, rebuilt a fresh unsigned-integer buffer using
numpy.frombuffer, reshape, and copy, then bit-cast back to BF16 before decoding. This distin-
guishes scoring quantized values from independently checking their byte reconstruction.

\section*{4.2 Source-field qualification diagnostics}

For each trajectory, the displacement rank-one statistic uses a float64 matrix with eleven frame rows
and columns formed by flattening all three displacement components over the full exported grid.
Each column is centered over time. The statistic is the largest eigenvalue of the temporal Gram
matrix divided by the sum of its eigenvalues, with a denominator minimum of 10\ensuremath{-}30. Reported
medians and p95 values aggregate these per-trajectory statistics, not individual voxels.
The relative energy residual is the absolute final residual divided by the sum of absolute adja-
cent changes in stored external work, with a denominator minimum of 10\ensuremath{-}12. The residual is the
change from the initial sum of kinetic energy, elastic energy, and accumulated plastic, damping,
and damage dissipation, minus accumulated external work. Its p95 and maximum are taken across
trajectories. Plastic-active and damage-active fractions count trajectories whose final full-domain
mean accumulated plastic strain exceeds 10\ensuremath{-}7 or whose final full-domain mean damage exceeds
10\ensuremath{-}9, respectively; their denominators are 2,000 pilot trajectories or 512 final holdout trajecto-
ries. Sample-contract failures count trajectories failing at least one source-array check, including
shape, finiteness, static density/material/mass, constructed field identities, irreversible ordering,
boundary/support conditions, internal force balance, cyclic loading, and nontrivial stress response.
Pilot feature diagnostics first average each frame and channel into 43 spatial blocks and divide
each channel by its trajectory-specific coarse-field RMS over time and space, floored at 10\ensuremath{-}8. The
feature vector concatenates each frame/channel’s spatial mean, spatial standard deviation, and
values at four fixed coarse-grid probes with zero-based (Z, Y, X) indices (0, 3, 1), (1, 2, 3), (2, 1, 0),
and (3, 0, 2). Across the 2,000 trajectories, feature columns are centered and standardized, retaining
only columns with standard deviation above 10\ensuremath{-}8. Normalized squared singular values define
variance fractions; the participation ratio is the reciprocal sum of their squares, and the component
counts are the smallest counts reaching 90\% and 99\% cumulative variance. The capacity check
tests exact collisions of parameter records and coarse fields rounded after multiplication by 256
and stored as signed 16-bit integers. These feature diagnostics differ from the physics audit’s
collision check on channel-scaled 83 block averages cast to float16. Source implementations and
saved measurements are preserved in Supplementary record S1.

\section*{4.4 Ordering and nonvacuity tests}

A decrement is counted when an adjacent decoded difference is below \ensuremath{-}10\ensuremath{-}8. On 512 trajectories,
ten adjacent transitions and 643 exported voxels give 1,342,177,280 comparisons per irreversible
channel. This is a counting denominator, not an independent statistical sample size.
All-frame pooled scores include frames 0–10. Dynamic and separate damage/plastic copying
comparisons use only frames 1–10, with corresponding denominators recomputed on that frame
range. Frozen acceptance requires dynamic error to be at most 0.95 times both true previous-frame
persistence and initial-frame copying; damage and plastic errors must separately beat previous-
frame persistence by that factor. The five magnitude thresholds are each 0.30, with zero tolerated
counted reversals and nonfinite values. Floor agreement is checked at absolute tolerance 10\ensuremath{-}10.
These criteria test reconstruction and declared ordering, not general mechanical admissibility.

The internal 800-trajectory test summarizes 8,800 supplied frames using the training script’s frame
metric, not Equation 2’s pooled channel-group statistic. Raw mean, median, p95, and maximum

the mean as uniformly accurate reconstruction.

\section*{6 Discussion and limitations}

The central result is a direct counterexample to identifying small field error with irreversible evo-
lution. A pooled norm measures discrepancy magnitudes, whereas irreversibility constrains signs
of adjacent changes. The same raw reconstructions exhibit acceptable pooled errors and many for-
bidden decreases. The decoded-only operation changes the admissible output set without changing
the learned representation. Its zero reversal counts are guaranteed, while its favorable magnitude
changes and copying comparisons are empirical observations on the tested fixture.
The constraint is deliberately narrow. It does not enforce density nonnegativity or mass con-
7

servation, categorical material identity, agreement between decoded velocities and displacement
derivatives, or consistency between the decoded von Mises channel and decoded stress tensor. Nor
does it impose decoded force balance, work balance, or a coupled constitutive evolution. Earlier
overestimates can be retained by cumulative maxima; monotonicity alone cannot correct an incor-
rect magnitude history. Active-region absolute errors and engineering observables needed to assess
such consequences were not established.
Physical interpretation is restricted by the normalized central-force family, lattice anisotropy,
native-axis versus canonical-frame interpretation, absence of independent material or reference-
solver calibration, and lack of resolution convergence. Interpolated neighboring voxels and succes-
sive frames are correlated. No confidence intervals or voxel-based significance claims are warranted
from the reported counts. One training seed and separately seeded holdout trajectories assess a
single trained codec within this generator family; they do not quantify training uncertainty, unseen-
material transfer, future-state forecasting, crack-growth accuracy, or fatigue life.
The evidence has distinct levels. Saved reports document the original experiment’s measured
results; captured code explains the operations; independent arithmetic checks confirm aggregate
relationships and links between source files and reports. None substitutes for fresh reproduction
using raw fields and checkpoint inference. The documented short in-memory parallel-production
benchmark does not demonstrate completed large-scale production written to persistent storage,
sustained storage bandwidth, or codec speedup, and is not used to support the scientific comparison.

\section*{7 Conclusion}

On the same 512 simulated trajectories, low reconstruction error through the BF16 token interface
coexisted with extensive raw damage and plastic-strain reversals. A decoded-only cumulative-
maximum contract eliminated these reversals by construction while retaining nontrivial magnitude
reconstruction and leaving other channels unchanged. The supported conclusion is reconstruction
with explicitly imposed irreversible ordering in a reduced lattice family. Learned irreversibility and
validated mechanics remain separate, unestablished claims.
