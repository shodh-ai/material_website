Metric semantics and geometry constraints in neural mixer inverse
design: a retrospective retained-artifact case study
Abstract
Neural inverse design can optimize a geometric surrogate without establishing the perfor-
mance of the resulting hard geometry. This case study separates geometric decoder objectives,
diﬀerentiable porous-ﬂow scores, hard-grid diagnostics, and a subsequently smoothed surface.
Stored numerical ﬁelds and meshes support a bounded explanation of their non-equivalence. The
geometric quantity called a thermal-dead-zone proxy is a squared occupancy-gradient penalty,
not a fraction of cold or stagnant ﬂuid cells. A volume-connected decoder reported zero proxy
penalty but 291810 thresholded solid components; its distinct hard-grid evaluation yielded a
cold/slow fraction of 0.3156383064. A diﬀerentiable optimizer subsequently reported a soft
diagnostic of 0.0777688250 and soft solid fraction of 0.2077771425. The saved output, hard-
thresholded and evaluated under the hard-grid protocol, instead yielded a cold/slow fraction
of 0.3029557981 and hard solid fraction of 0.1528625488. These are diﬀerent observables and
protocols, not a calibrated soft-to-hard error estimate. Surface smoothing preserved triangle in-
dices but reduced measured area by 75.8632232% and retained 9070 boundary edges. The earlier
ﬂow evaluation therefore does not certify the smoothed surface. The contribution is a retained-
artifact account of why objective satisfaction, hard-grid diagnostics, and surface qualiﬁcation
must remain separate scientiﬁc claims.
1 Introduction and contributions
Inverse design of a mixer requires a chain of statements: a representation must describe material,
an objective must measure a relevant property, a forward calculation must evaluate the proposed
geometry, and the exported surface must retain the properties attributed to it. A small objective
value at one stage does not establish the next statement. This issue is particularly important when
the same descriptive label is attached to a geometric penalty and to a ﬁeld-derived fraction.
The present case study examines a frozen-foundation geometric decoder, a manufacturability
variant, a volume-connected variant, diﬀerentiable optimizers V1 and V2, a hard-grid audit, and a
smoothed ﬁnal mesh. These names identify computational stages, not certiﬁcations. The central
contribution is a bounded retrospective explanation, supported by remeasurement of retained ﬁelds
and surfaces, of why a geometric proxy or soft optimization score cannot certify a hard-geometry
mixer.
Three distinctions organize the analysis. First, an occupancy-gradient penalty contains no ve-
locity or temperature observation. Second, a sigmoid-weighted porous diagnostic and a percentile-
thresholded hard-ﬂuid fraction have diﬀerent geometry, thresholds, and denominators. Third, un-
changed surface indexing does not imply unchanged geometry or validated ﬂow after smoothing.
The results also expose dependence between nominal candidate cases and a mismatch between the
timing of logged optimizer metrics and exported geometry. No new diﬀerentiable-physics method
or physically validated mixer is claimed.

2 Related work
Holl et al. describe diﬀerentiable PDE planning and control, including problem-dependent obser-
vations and a combination of observation and control objectives [ 1]. Their formulation makes the
chosen observation map and loss part of the task deﬁnition. This is relevant to interpreting an
inverse-design score: diﬀerentiation provides sensitivity of the speciﬁed calculation, not an auto-
matic interpretation of its objective as an experimentally measured property. Their study does not
benchmark the mixer examined here.
Papadopoulos and Suli analyze a constrained material-distribution problem for Stokes ﬂow [ 2]. In
the supplied author manuscript dated March 27, 2022, the formulation includes a volume constraint
and the analysis establishes conditional ﬁnite-element convergence toward isolated local minimiz-
ers. Their material variable near one permits ﬂuid ﬂow; the occupancy used here near one denotes
solid. Moreover, their constrained Stokes problem is not the periodic, ﬁnite-time lattice-Boltzmann
calculation, soft-penalty optimization, or surface-export sequence examined below. Its convergence
results cannot certify this pipeline. These works provide methodological context rather than mea-
sured baselines, calibration references, or evidence against diﬀerentiable simulation and topology
optimization in general.
3 Scientiﬁc methods and validation protocols
3.1 Retrospective scope and geometric representation
The analysis uses captured source, saved reports and optimization histories, original numerical
arrays, and the actual unsmoothed and smoothed OBJ meshes. Local checking scripts reopened
the stored arrays and meshes, recomputed scalar diagnostics and triangle geometry, and compared
retained ﬁelds elementwise. No original neural inference, checkpoint execution, optimizer execution,
or ﬂow simulation was rerun. Thus, remeasurement establishes properties of retained artifacts,
while reports and source establish the recorded computational procedure. Exact checking inputs
and results are preserved in Supplementary record S1.
The frozen-foundation bridge reports 3337537 trainable decoder parameters and zero trainable
foundation parameters. Arithmetic from the captured decoder architecture independently repro-
duces the decoder count. This supports the architecture description, but not a replayed binding
to the original foundation checkpoint or a claim about its predictive utility. The decoder maps
conditioning tokens and a pooled embedding to a scalar ﬁeld called a signed distance ﬁeld (SDF).
This designation alone does not establish the signed-distance property. Negative values denote solid
for hard thresholding. Source-supported interpolation and ﬁeld calculations use ﬂoat32; decoder
source also permits optional bﬂoat16 autocasting and enables TF32 in relevant routines. Historical
precision ﬂags and complete launch bindings are not established by source defaults.

4 Experimental results
4.1 Decoder stages and hard-grid dependence
The original geometric decoder and the volume-connected variant both report zero geometric dead-
zone penalty. The manufacturability variant instead collapses soft solid volume to 0.0008011368.
The volume-connected variant retains soft volume 0.2541742325 but reports 291810 solid com-
ponents under the stated threshold and face connectivity. Its occupancy-gradient measure is
0.4074925184, comfortably exceeding the geometric penalty threshold. This combination is consis-

5 Discussion and limitations
The observed failures occur at diﬀerent logical interfaces. The geometric decoder can satisfy an
area-like threshold through occupancy variation without producing connected material. The man-
ufacturability variant demonstrates a diﬀerent failure, loss of almost all soft solid volume despite
soft regularization. The volume-connected variant recovers material but not hard connectivity.
These results concern the particular objectives and retained stages, rather than an intrinsic failure
of topology optimization.
Diﬀerentiable optimization changes the observation contract. Its smooth diagnostic weights cold-
ness, slowness, and ﬂuid occupancy continuously, whereas the hard diagnostic counts cells below
a relative speed percentile. Even with an identical velocity ﬁeld these deﬁnitions need not agree.
Porous population blending, porosity-weighted velocity, diﬀerent scalar advection, and unbound

historical horizons add further distinctions. Consequently, neither subtracting the two diagnostic
values nor treating their ratio as an error estimate would be scientiﬁcally justiﬁed.
The pressure scale illustrates the same problem. A numerical target and a factor of 105 do
not establish a physical pressure unit. The retained calculations support ﬁnite-time scaled lattice-
pressure and scalar-ﬁeld diagnostics under the speciﬁed periodic convention. They do not establish
ﬂow convergence, dimensional material properties, independently calibrated speed, temperature
or time, residence time, species mixing, chemical yield, or plant performance. Physical validation
would require a speciﬁed physical domain, boundary conditions, dimensional mapping, convergence
assessment, and comparison with suitable observations.
Retained-artifact remeasurement strengthens the account beyond report-only interpretation: ar-
rays were actually reopened, diagnostics recomputed, duplicate ﬁelds identiﬁed, and mesh geometry
measured. It cannot reconstruct missing historical bindings. The original checkpoint execution,
authenticated launch arguments, matched-start identity, and full precision conﬁguration remain
unresolved. The replica-selected histories also provide no independent trajectory ensemble from
which to estimate uncertainty.
Finally, surface smoothing is a scientiﬁc transformation, not merely a presentation change. A sub-
stantial area reduction and open boundary edges prevent assigning the earlier voxel-ﬂow results to
the smoothed surface. Mesh-to-grid re-extraction, after-smoothing ﬂow evaluation, self-intersection
testing, and manufacturing-tolerance assessment would be necessary before advancing stronger ge-
ometry claims.
6 Conclusion
This retrospective case study separates three non-equivalent quantities: a squared geometric penalty,
a porous sigmoid-weighted cold/slow score, and a hard-ﬂuid percentile diagnostic. Their values
cannot certify one another. The volume-connected decoder’s zero proxy penalty coexists with frag-
mented thresholded material and a nonzero hard diagnostic. Diﬀerentiable optimizer V2 preserves
more soft occupancy than V1 in its recorded run, but its logged metrics do not directly describe
the exported post-update geometry or the ﬁnal smoothed mesh.
The strongest supported endpoint is the saved V2 hard-grid state: its retained ﬁelds yield a
cold/slow fraction of 0.3029557981 and hard solid fraction of 0.1528625488 under the stated ﬁnite-
time numerical protocol. The smoothed surface remains open and lacks an after-smoothing forward
evaluation. The general lesson is to bind every performance statement to its observable, geometry,
denominator, boundary convention, numerical horizon, and actual saved output.
