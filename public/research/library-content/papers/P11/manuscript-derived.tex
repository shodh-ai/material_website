Metric semantics and geometry constraints in neural mixer inverse
design: a retrospective retained-artifact case study

\section*{Abstract}

Neural inverse design can optimize a geometric surrogate without establishing the perfor-
mance of the resulting hard geometry. This case study separates geometric decoder objectives,
differentiable porous-flow scores, hard-grid diagnostics, and a subsequently smoothed surface.
Stored numerical fields and meshes support a bounded explanation of their non-equivalence. The
geometric quantity called a thermal-dead-zone proxy is a squared occupancy-gradient penalty,
not a fraction of cold or stagnant fluid cells. A volume-connected decoder reported zero proxy
penalty but 291810 thresholded solid components; its distinct hard-grid evaluation yielded a
cold/slow fraction of 0.3156383064. A differentiable optimizer subsequently reported a soft
diagnostic of 0.0777688250 and soft solid fraction of 0.2077771425. The saved output, hard-
thresholded and evaluated under the hard-grid protocol, instead yielded a cold/slow fraction
of 0.3029557981 and hard solid fraction of 0.1528625488. These are different observables and
protocols, not a calibrated soft-to-hard error estimate. Surface smoothing preserved triangle in-
dices but reduced measured area by 75.8632232\% and retained 9070 boundary edges. The earlier
flow evaluation therefore does not certify the smoothed surface. The contribution is a retained-
artifact account of why objective satisfaction, hard-grid diagnostics, and surface qualification
must remain separate scientific claims.

\section*{1 Introduction and contributions}

Inverse design of a mixer requires a chain of statements: a representation must describe material,
an objective must measure a relevant property, a forward calculation must evaluate the proposed
geometry, and the exported surface must retain the properties attributed to it. A small objective
value at one stage does not establish the next statement. This issue is particularly important when
the same descriptive label is attached to a geometric penalty and to a field-derived fraction.
The present case study examines a frozen-foundation geometric decoder, a manufacturability
variant, a volume-connected variant, differentiable optimizers V1 and V2, a hard-grid audit, and a
smoothed final mesh. These names identify computational stages, not certifications. The central
contribution is a bounded retrospective explanation, supported by remeasurement of retained fields
and surfaces, of why a geometric proxy or soft optimization score cannot certify a hard-geometry
mixer.
Three distinctions organize the analysis. First, an occupancy-gradient penalty contains no ve-
locity or temperature observation. Second, a sigmoid-weighted porous diagnostic and a percentile-
thresholded hard-fluid fraction have different geometry, thresholds, and denominators. Third, un-
changed surface indexing does not imply unchanged geometry or validated flow after smoothing.
The results also expose dependence between nominal candidate cases and a mismatch between the
timing of logged optimizer metrics and exported geometry. No new differentiable-physics method
or physically validated mixer is claimed.

\section*{2 Related work}

Holl et al. describe differentiable PDE planning and control, including problem-dependent obser-
vations and a combination of observation and control objectives [ 1]. Their formulation makes the
chosen observation map and loss part of the task definition. This is relevant to interpreting an
inverse-design score: differentiation provides sensitivity of the specified calculation, not an auto-
matic interpretation of its objective as an experimentally measured property. Their study does not
benchmark the mixer examined here.
Papadopoulos and Suli analyze a constrained material-distribution problem for Stokes flow [ 2]. In
the supplied author manuscript dated March 27, 2022, the formulation includes a volume constraint
and the analysis establishes conditional finite-element convergence toward isolated local minimiz-
ers. Their material variable near one permits fluid flow; the occupancy used here near one denotes
solid. Moreover, their constrained Stokes problem is not the periodic, finite-time lattice-Boltzmann
calculation, soft-penalty optimization, or surface-export sequence examined below. Its convergence
results cannot certify this pipeline. These works provide methodological context rather than mea-
sured baselines, calibration references, or evidence against differentiable simulation and topology
optimization in general.

\section*{3.1 Retrospective scope and geometric representation}

The analysis uses captured source, saved reports and optimization histories, original numerical
arrays, and the actual unsmoothed and smoothed OBJ meshes. Local checking scripts reopened
the stored arrays and meshes, recomputed scalar diagnostics and triangle geometry, and compared
retained fields elementwise. No original neural inference, checkpoint execution, optimizer execution,
or flow simulation was rerun. Thus, remeasurement establishes properties of retained artifacts,
while reports and source establish the recorded computational procedure. Exact checking inputs
and results are preserved in Supplementary record S1.
The frozen-foundation bridge reports 3337537 trainable decoder parameters and zero trainable
foundation parameters. Arithmetic from the captured decoder architecture independently repro-

to the original foundation checkpoint or a claim about its predictive utility. The decoder maps
conditioning tokens and a pooled embedding to a scalar field called a signed distance field (SDF).

for hard thresholding. Source-supported interpolation and field calculations use float32; decoder

precision flags and complete launch bindings are not established by source defaults.

\section*{5 Discussion and limitations}

The observed failures occur at different logical interfaces. The geometric decoder can satisfy an
area-like threshold through occupancy variation without producing connected material. The man-
ufacturability variant demonstrates a different failure, loss of almost all soft solid volume despite
soft regularization. The volume-connected variant recovers material but not hard connectivity.
These results concern the particular objectives and retained stages, rather than an intrinsic failure
of topology optimization.
Differentiable optimization changes the observation contract. Its smooth diagnostic weights cold-
ness, slowness, and fluid occupancy continuously, whereas the hard diagnostic counts cells below
a relative speed percentile. Even with an identical velocity field these definitions need not agree.
Porous population blending, porosity-weighted velocity, different scalar advection, and unbound
7

historical horizons add further distinctions. Consequently, neither subtracting the two diagnostic
values nor treating their ratio as an error estimate would be scientifically justified.
The pressure scale illustrates the same problem. A numerical target and a factor of 105 do
not establish a physical pressure unit. The retained calculations support finite-time scaled lattice-
pressure and scalar-field diagnostics under the specified periodic convention. They do not establish
flow convergence, dimensional material properties, independently calibrated speed, temperature
or time, residence time, species mixing, chemical yield, or plant performance. Physical validation
would require a specified physical domain, boundary conditions, dimensional mapping, convergence
assessment, and comparison with suitable observations.
Retained-artifact remeasurement strengthens the account beyond report-only interpretation: ar-
rays were actually reopened, diagnostics recomputed, duplicate fields identified, and mesh geometry
measured. It cannot reconstruct missing historical bindings. The original checkpoint execution,
authenticated launch arguments, matched-start identity, and full precision configuration remain
unresolved. The replica-selected histories also provide no independent trajectory ensemble from
which to estimate uncertainty.
Finally, surface smoothing is a scientific transformation, not merely a presentation change. A sub-
stantial area reduction and open boundary edges prevent assigning the earlier voxel-flow results to
the smoothed surface. Mesh-to-grid re-extraction, after-smoothing flow evaluation, self-intersection
testing, and manufacturing-tolerance assessment would be necessary before advancing stronger ge-
ometry claims.

\section*{6 Conclusion}

This retrospective case study separates three non-equivalent quantities: a squared geometric penalty,
a porous sigmoid-weighted cold/slow score, and a hard-fluid percentile diagnostic. Their values
cannot certify one another. The volume-connected decoder’s zero proxy penalty coexists with frag-
mented thresholded material and a nonzero hard diagnostic. Differentiable optimizer V2 preserves
more soft occupancy than V1 in its recorded run, but its logged metrics do not directly describe
the exported post-update geometry or the final smoothed mesh.
The strongest supported endpoint is the saved V2 hard-grid state: its retained fields yield a
cold/slow fraction of 0.3029557981 and hard solid fraction of 0.1528625488 under the stated finite-
time numerical protocol. The smoothed surface remains open and lacks an after-smoothing forward
evaluation. The general lesson is to bind every performance statement to its observable, geometry,
denominator, boundary convention, numerical horizon, and actual saved output.
