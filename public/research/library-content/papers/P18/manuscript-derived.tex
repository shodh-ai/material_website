Reconstruction, symmetry response, and reference stability in an
inherited Fe–Cr geometry tokenizer

\section*{Abstract}

Accurate reconstruction of atomistic coordinates does not by itself establish Euclidean equiv-
ariance or physical accuracy. We characterize these distinctions for an inherited continuous
Fe–Cr geometry tokenizer using three separate evidence sets: retained grouped-holdout evalu-
ation summaries, saved coordinates and transform diagnostics for one synthetic vacancy crop,
and saved arrays and logs from a separate electronic-structure reference ladder. The native
representation contains 192 continuous values per atom, comprising 32 carrier values and 160
learned values. The original evaluation reports a median and 95th percentile of reconstruc-
tion root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of 0.00687997 and 0.00861934 Å on 731 correlated
patches from 120 held-out physical parents. Each defect family has a lower reported median
error than both a zero-learned-lane control and a relaxed-shadow predictor. Independent re-
duction of saved base coordinates gives 0.02956387 Å RMSD for one synthetic 100-atom crop;
the reported off-axis rotation-equivariance error is 0.03654489 Å, with only its core/context
aggregation checked independently. These singleton measurements are not population esti-
mates. Separately, four self-consistent Quantum ESPRESSO calculations on one periodic 16-
atom Fe–Cr component fail parameter-stability comparisons: changing smearing produces an
energy difference of 0.00188310 eV/atom and maximum absolute force- and stress-component
differences of 0.02231615 eV/Å and 0.347 GPa, respectively. An independent magnetic ini-
tialization does not reach a converged self-consistent solution. The results support a bounded
geometry-reconstruction finding, not a physically qualified force model, and demonstrate why
reconstruction, symmetry response, numerical agreement, and reference stability require distinct
validation.

\section*{1 Introduction and contributions}

Atomistic representations are often assessed by their ability to reconstruct coordinates. For defect-
containing alloys, however, several scientifically different requirements can be conflated: recovering a
particular configuration, responding consistently to rotations and translations, preserving chemical
information, and supporting accurate energies or forces. A small coordinate error addresses the first
requirement under the evaluated sampling and correspondence conventions. It does not establish
the others.
This paper examines an inherited Fe–Cr geometry tokenizer trained on local environments con-
taining C15 self-interstitial clusters, 1
2 \ensuremath{\langle}111\ensuremath{\rangle} interstitial loops, and spherical voids. The architecture
is not introduced here. Our contribution is a retrospective empirical characterization that sepa-
rates three questions supported by different records. First, what do the original parent-grouped
reconstruction summaries establish? Second, what do saved coordinates and reported transforma-
tion diagnostics establish for one subsequently constructed synthetic crop? Third, is a separately
calculated physical reference stable enough to support an accuracy comparison?
1

These questions have different populations and chronology. The original August 2026 dataset
contains 8,000 patches, with 731 test patches derived from 120 physical parents. The October 9
synthetic diagnostic uses one nonperiodic 100-atom vacancy crop, not a new molecular-dynamics
trajectory or a sample from the original test population. The October 10 reference ladder uses
one periodic 16-atom Fe–Cr component. Its energies, forces, and stresses are not predictions of the
geometry decoder and cannot explain the crop’s reconstruction errors.
The focused finding is that favorable grouped-holdout geometry summaries coexist with a nonzero
reported transformation residual and with an independently unstable physical reference. We retain
the original reconstruction result at its reported evidence level, distinguish independently reduced
saved-coordinate errors from retained transform scalars, and report the failed reference comparisons
without interpreting numerical consistency as physical qualification.

\section*{2 Related work}

Euclidean-equivariant networks impose transformation laws on their outputs, rather than merely
minimizing reconstruction error on a coordinate distribution. Satorras and colleagues formulate
translation, rotation/reflection, and permutation equivariance explicitly and introduce an equiv-
ariant graph-network architecture [ 2]. This supplies the relevant conceptual distinction: for a
reconstruction map F and geometric action g, equivariance concerns F (gX ) = gF (X), whereas re-
construction concerns F (X) \ensuremath{\approx} X. A network described in inherited source comments as belonging
to an EGNN family need not inherit the transformation guarantees of that published architecture.
The inspected Fe–Cr implementation also uses absolute fractional-coordinate Fourier features and
a transformer decoder; its behavior must therefore be characterized directly rather than inferred
from a family name.
Quantum ESPRESSO provides plane-wave and pseudopotential electronic-structure methods,
including self-consistent calculations through PWscf [ 1]. That software context does not certify any
particular private calculation. Self-consistent-field (SCF) convergence at fixed numerical parameters
and stability of energies, forces, and stresses against those parameters are separate tests. The
reference ladder below examines this distinction using the recorded Quantum ESPRESSO 7.5 inputs
and outputs.
The scope here is specific to Fe–Cr defect geometry and a separate alloy reference calculation.
The inherited carrier/learned-lane layout and encoder–decoder design are also used in the private
companion antibody analysis; they are not architectural contributions of this paper. Companion
analyses of antibody poses and molecular rotational stability concern different systems and mea-
sured outcomes, not independent replications of the Fe–Cr results. We neither infer architectural
priority nor claim an exhaustive comparison across those studies.

\section*{3.2 Original preparation and crop construction}

The inherited preparation protocol uses a lattice constant of 2.8271412248 Å, C15 parent repetitions
of 10 \ensuremath{\times} 10 \ensuremath{\times} 10, and extended-defect repetitions of 16 \ensuremath{\times} 16 \ensuremath{\times} 16. Temperatures span 300–1100 K,
absolute strain is at most 0.01, and chromium fractions span 0.03–0.15 overall, with C15 sampling
restricted to 0.03–0.10. C15 networks use merged motifs with 4, 6, 8, 10, or 12 net interstitials.
Loops use an Atomsk Volterra prismatic construction with Poisson ratio 0.33 and radii of 6–12 Å;
post-relaxation dislocation analysis supplies anchors. Voids have radii of 4.5–9 Å and use relaxed
cavity-surface anchors.
The specified dynamics use a 1 fs step, 5,000 equilibration steps, and 20,000 production steps.
Forty snapshots are saved at 500-step intervals; PyMBAR equilibration detection and conservative
correlated-data subsampling select two or three frames per parent. Each selected frame is indepen-
dently quenched to produce a relaxed shadow. Crops are extracted without isolated-crop relaxation,
with a 4.5 Å core radius, a 9 Å crop radius, and at most 256 atoms. The inherited screening requires
finite hot and shadow payloads, minimum interatomic separation of 1.5 Å, matching Wigner–Seitz
net defect counts, and a shadow-force threshold recorded as 0.02 eV/Å. That preparation threshold
is distinct from the explicitly componentwise force differences used for the reference ladder below.
The retained environment records list LAMMPS 2025.7.22.2.0, PyMBAR 4.0.3, and OVITO 3.12.0.
C15 topology is assessed on quenched Wigner–Seitz interstitial dumbbells, using a 2.5 Å connec-
tion cutoff, 12◦ orientation tolerance, and 15◦ bond-angle and plane tolerances. Required fractions
are 0.75 for \ensuremath{\langle}110\ensuremath{\rangle} dumbbells, 0.70 for the largest connected physical component, 0.25 for C15 bonds
among physical connections, and 0.80 for plane matches. For expected net interstitial count n, the
measured dumbbell count must be at least n, and the minimum C15 bond count is max(2, \ensuremath{\lceil}0.25n\ensuremath{\rceil}).
Loop screening requires a closed dislocation line of at least 10 Å and Burgers-vector component
0.5 within 0.15; void screening requires preserved vacancy count. These are inherited preparation
criteria, not newly established physical accuracy.

\section*{3.3 Representation, adapter, and inherited network}

Each atom has a continuous 192-value token: a 32-value carrier and a 160-value learned lane.
There is no discrete codebook. A 5,120-dimensional linear lift appears separately in the source as
a downstream interface, not the native representation measured here. Exact-header information
includes atomic numbers, valid count, masks, local box, parent cell, and branch identity. The
carrier also stores coordinates. Consequently, carrier round-trip accuracy must not be mistaken for
learned reconstruction.

\section*{3.4 Training, selection, and original criteria}

The inherited run trains from scratch for 12,000 steps with AdamW, learning rate 3 \ensuremath{\times} 10\ensuremath{-}4, weight
decay 10\ensuremath{-}4, gradient clipping at norm 1, and seed 20260708. The schedule uses 500 warm-up steps
followed by cosine decay toward 3\ensuremath{\times}10\ensuremath{-}6. Eight H100 GPUs use NCCL distributed data parallelism,
per-rank batch 4, and global batch 32. Validation occurs every 250 steps; the checkpoint criterion

statements describe the inspected code and recorded protocol, not a replay of historical execution.
For this branch, the loss comprises mean squared Cartesian position error over active valid atoms

source explicitly converts network coordinate inputs to single precision and evaluates coordinate

\section*{5 Discussion and limitations}

The original result is a favorable reconstruction summary under a specific parent-grouped split and
nonperiodic local-box convention. Keeping all patches from one physical parent together avoids one
important leakage route. It does not make patches statistically independent, nor does it establish
transfer beyond the sampled parent construction, compositions, temperatures, defect sizes, and
crop protocol. The zero-lane control supports dependence on the learned representation, while
the shadow control distinguishes reconstruction of hot coordinates from prediction by a quenched
structure. Neither control establishes a general-purpose atomistic model.
The synthetic diagnostic addresses a different limitation. Its base errors can be independently
recovered from saved XYZ arrays, but it contains only one constructed configuration. Its reported
nonzero rotation residual is evidence against treating the recorded implementation as exactly equiv-
ariant under that diagnostic; it is not an independently remeasured transformed-coordinate result or
a population rate of symmetry failure. Absolute-coordinate features, header conditioning, canonical
8

ordering, and learned decoding must be considered together when interpreting symmetry behavior.
We do not identify a causal mechanism for the measured residual from these records.
Exact species and count recovery has a narrower meaning than chemical reconstruction. The de-
coder receives atomic-number header information, and the projection enforces known composition.
Preserving counts preserves total mass only when the same fixed species-mass table is used. There
is no decoded velocity or energy channel, so neither momentum nor energy conservation follows.
Force labels retained alongside coordinates are not force predictions. No dynamics-stability claim
is supported.
Physical-reference stability is another independent prerequisite. The 16-atom ladder demon-
strates failures of the chosen numerical settings to satisfy the fixed stability limits, even where
SCF converges. Because the potential/reference fixture is not the synthetic crop and the decoder
does not predict forces, these failures neither cause nor quantify geometry reconstruction error.
They prevent the separate potential comparison from serving as qualified physical accuracy. The
recorded physical-qualification protocol requires 24 qualifying complex-defect reference pairs. The
available records do not supply these pairs, and the component calculation does not meet that
coverage requirement.
Historical preparation checks also require their original scope. An earlier C15 evaluation failed
under connectedness and fixed-radius observables later identified in the records as invalid. Inde-
pendently calibrated dumbbell-topology criteria were subsequently applied to new target parents,
rather than retroactively converting those failures into successes. The historical new-target sum-
mary reports 148 of 150 frames passing; a later replay reports 147 of 150, with three shadow-force
failures. Both retain 50 passing parents under a requirement of at least two passing frames per
parent. These distinct frame counts are not silently reconciled, no cause is inferred, and neither is
used as current physical qualification.
The supplied reductions report passing results for 150 saved-summary checks, 10 manifest checks,
58 synthetic-coordinate checks, and 335 reference-array/log checks within their declared scopes, in-
cluding checks of the arithmetic underlying the reference failures. These checks establish numerical
consistency and traceability, not physical validity. They do not reproduce original holdout in-
ference, remeasure transformed decoded coordinates, establish parameter-converged references, or
provide complex-defect coverage. The original HDF5 dataset and checkpoint were not opened in
this work, so the original test reconstruction remains a reported measurement rather than a newly
reproduced one.

\section*{6 Conclusion}

The inherited Fe–Cr tokenizer has retained test reconstruction RMSD median and 95th percentile
of 0.00687997 and 0.00861934 Å on 731 patches from 120 held-out parents. Family-specific controls
support the original geometry-reconstruction result within that dataset and protocol. Independent
reduction of a separate synthetic crop’s saved base coordinates gives 0.02956387 Å RMSD, while
retained transform diagnostics report 0.03654489 Å off-axis rotation-equivariance error. The crop
provides a bounded diagnostic, not a generalization estimate or a new threshold test.
A separate 16-atom reference ladder fails energy/force/stress parameter-stability comparisons
despite four converged SCF calculations; an independent magnetic initialization remains noncon-
verged. Accordingly, the available evidence supports geometry characterization, not qualified physi-
cal prediction. The scientific significance is the explicit separation of reconstruction, transformation

response, numerical consistency, and physical-reference adequacy in evaluating an inherited atom-
istic representation.
