Separate Gates for Reconstruction, Symmetry and Population Size
in an Adsorbate–Slab Codec

\section*{Abstract}

Scientific qualification of an atomistic codec requires more than a small reconstruction me-
dian. We reconcile retained evaluations of an adsorbate–slab representation while separating
a historical checkpoint, a later two-seed pilot, a source-expansion audit and a fresh single-case
symmetry diagnostic. The shared 200-entry pilot holdout reports learned stored-byte median
atom errors of 0.00842 and 0.00854 Å, but 95th percentiles of 0.187 and 0.167 Å and maxima
near 19 Å. The checked cache identifiers match the complete pilot manifest. Reaggregation of
all 672583 retained duplicate groups reconciles 200079631 distinct frames from the reported
upstream count, leaving a 799920369-frame shortfall against the frozen target. The historical
checkpoint separately fails a saved rigid-transform test whose original numeric input and ap-
plied transform we independently verify. Its scalar is an energy-trace difference rather than
an adsorption label, and its species checks compare inputs rather than decoded conservation.
These results distinguish median reconstruction, tail robustness, symmetry and source capacity
without claiming new neural inference or physical certification.

\section*{1 Introduction}

An atomistic representation can reconstruct many coordinates accurately without preserving every
property needed for scientific use. Median reconstruction, large-error tails, symmetry, energy defi-
nitions, and the population available for training answer different questions. A successful result on
one of these axes does not determine the others. This distinction is particularly consequential when
a learned representation accompanies explicit atomic and cell information: exact recovery through
the explicit lane need not demonstrate accuracy of the learned decoder.
We examine retained artifacts from an adsorbate–slab codec and its subsequent evaluation cam-
paigns. The study separates four bodies of evidence: a July 2026 historical checkpoint evaluation,
a later two-seed pilot drawn from an OC20-Dense-derived cache, a source-expansion capacity as-
sessment, and an October single-case rigid-transform diagnostic of the historical checkpoint. These
are neither interchangeable cohorts nor stages of one continuously improving model.
The contribution is a retrospective reconciliation of what each evaluation supports. The pilot
reports small median errors after a stored-precision round trip, but substantial tails remain. The
expansion campaign fails its frozen population target even after duplicate accounting is reconciled.
A separately recorded symmetry counterexample survives independent checking of its raw input
and applied transform. This combination identifies a concrete qualification gap between typical
reconstruction accuracy, robustness of the decoded representation, and the size of its admissible
physical population. It does not introduce a new codec, retrain an existing one, or independently
reproduce its neural predictions.

\section*{2 Scientific context}

OC20 distinguishes prediction of energy and forces from prediction of relaxed structures or relaxed
energies, and defines separate evaluation distributions [ 1]. These task definitions matter here: an
error in stored coordinate reconstruction is not, by itself, an error measure for a catalyst-screening
task. Our private pilot also uses its own material-group split and cannot be treated as an official
OC20 benchmark submission.
The inspected first AdsorbML preprint defines adsorption energy through the combined system,
clean slab, and gas reference, filters physically unsuitable relaxed configurations, and uses DFT
follow-up to evaluate candidate structures [ 2]. We use this distinction to interpret the scalar
quantities in our retained records. A change along a single energy trace does not provide the
required component references. The same preprint specifies RPBE/V ASP calculations; a result
from a different electronic-structure method would require a method-equivalence argument before
a numerical discrepancy could be interpreted as an adsorption-energy error. These references
provide task and method context, not external validation of our codec or its reported performance.

\section*{3 Materials and methods}


\section*{3.1 Evidence and cohort separation}

The retained material includes complete pilot identifiers and cache files, original aggregate evalu-
ation reports, source snapshots, frozen acceptance specifications, the complete retained duplicate-
group table and digest list, and the raw numeric file used by the later symmetry diagnostic. Private
supplementary record S1 preserves these originals and the exact local checking inputs and results.
Source programs were inspected as text. No retrieved application, serialized model, training pro-
cedure, or DFT calculation was executed in this analysis.
The historical evaluation concerns a checkpoint selected at training step 24000 and reports 598
selection-validation and 469 representative-validation examples. The prospective pilot uses 1600
training, 100 selection, 100 representative-validation, and 200 holdout entries, with two indepen-
dently recorded training seeds. The 200-entry holdout is shared by the two seeds; the seeds are
not independent datasets. The expansion campaign counts physical frames rather than trajectory
identifiers. Finally, the October diagnostic contains one fresh input, evaluated against the historical
checkpoint; it does not rerun that checkpoint’s held-out cohort.

The codec represents each atom by 192 components: a 32-component explicit carrier and a 160-
component learned lane. The carrier stores initial fractional coordinates, Cartesian displacements
for four selected trajectory frames, and species and configuration information. A separate header
supplies atomic numbers, the full cell, atom masks and branch identity. The encoder receives
species, fractional-coordinate Fourier features, folded displacements, masks, cell features, branch
identity and the normalized target scalar. A message-passing encoder produces bounded per-atom
learned features; a set-transformer decoder consumes these features and header information, but
not the carrier coordinates or displacements. It reconstructs initial fractional coordinates, folded
Cartesian displacements and the scalar. The carried-coordinate control instead reads the explicit
fractional coordinates and multiplies them by the target cell. This is an autoencoding comparison,
not geometry generation from an information-free input, and the 160-component learned lane is
not a compressive representation of the raw atomic degrees of freedom.
The same historical branch-trainer snapshot is retained as method evidence in a companion an-
tibody pose and reconstruction study. This is shared implementation lineage, not evidence of a
common checkpoint execution or measured population. The antibody cohorts, historical adsorbate
branch and later OC20 pilot retain separate empirical and source-version scopes; the pilot modi-
fications are described below. Source identity and companion-version bindings are retained with
Supplementary record S1.

\section*{3.3 Population accounting}

The expansion specification defines its target as one billion distinct physical frames in its declared
source scope. Physical-frame identity is defined by a canonical payload containing species, periodic-
ity, the full cell and wrapped fractional coordinates. The admitted convention requires all three axes
to be periodic. Cell coefficients are quantized at 10\ensuremath{-}6 Å and fractional coordinates at 10\ensuremath{-}7. Canon-
icalization removes atom-order, integer-lattice-wrapping and global periodic-origin differences, but
does not canonicalize lattice-basis changes or rotated-cell representations. Finite quantization can
merge extremely close geometries: exact comparison means equality of these quantized canonical
payloads, not equality of unquantized coordinates or general physical equivalence. Distinct frames
within a trajectory may remain correlated. A hash-grouping stage identifies candidate duplicate
groups; the saved exact canonical-payload comparisons assign each group a distinct-payload count.
For reported upstream frame count N , group sizes rg and retained distinct-payload counts dg, the

\section*{3.4 Rigid-input diagnostic}

The raw numeric archive contains 11 saved position frames for 57 atoms. The diagnostic selects four
frames using a rounded, evenly spaced index grid, giving indices 0, 3, 7, 10. Its transform rotates
about the measured slab normal by 47 degrees and translates by (1.5, 0, 0) Å. With row-vector
coordinates X, cell matrix C, proper rotation R and translation t, the implemented transformation
is
X \ensuremath{\prime} = XR T + t, C \ensuremath{\prime} = CR T, \ensuremath{\Delta}X \ensuremath{\prime} = \ensuremath{\Delta}XR T. (4)
The cell and vectors transform together. The diagnostic wraps the first two fractional axes and
leaves the surface-normal axis nonperiodic. We independently reproduce this input operation and
its periodic pair-distance comparison from the original arrays. This tests the validity of the applied
input transform; it does not reproduce the learned output or infer invariance under every possible
rigid motion.

\section*{4 Results}


\section*{4.1 A separated pilot with small medians and large tails}

All 2000 pilot entries have distinct trajectory identifiers, source hashes and semantic-token hashes.
They represent 763 system identifiers and 676 material identifiers. None of the five recorded iden-
tity keys is shared across splits. The four cache byte hashes match the pilot audit. The checked
trajectory, system, material and source-hash fields match their manifest rows. The checked cell,
reference-energy, shift and recorded-frame metadata are finite and structurally consistent. These
findings establish the retained identity and metadata record, while the object-array geometry re-
mains outside independent readback.
The holdout contains 19601 atoms and the training manifest contains 122777 atoms, matching
the reported denominators. Both saved seed reports pass their declared median-based stored-byte

\section*{4.2 Historical results are not a new held-out test}

The historical checkpoint’s representative-validation report gives adsorbate-trajectory median er-
ror 0.0635430 Å, 95th percentile 7.24637 Å, and sample-maximum-error 95th percentile 0.325931 Å.
The October audit compares these values with its declared limits of 0.05, 0.10 and 0.25 Å, respec-
tively. All three comparisons fail. The October records reference the original July report, so this is
a retrospective classification against later declared gates, not a freshly executed historical holdout.
The historical all-atom median and these adsorbate-trajectory statistics also have different popu-
lations and definitions; they cannot be substituted for one another. The later pilot uses different
checkpoints and does not retrospectively repair the historical evaluation.

\section*{5 Discussion and limitations}

The evaluations support a layered interpretation. The prospective pilot demonstrates a reported
improvement in typical reconstruction under its specific stored-precision and split protocol. It also
exposes substantial tails that the median gate does not constrain. Population sufficiency fails a
separate, prespecified target, regardless of the pilot medians. The historical checkpoint’s saved
rigid-transform result identifies a different vulnerability, supported by independently checked input
geometry but not by new model inference. Pooling these findings into a single success or failure
score would discard their scientific content.
The practical distinction is between a gate and the property it measures. A reconstruction cri-
terion should name its population and statistic. An energy criterion should identify its physical
target and component references. A symmetry criterion should transform cell, coordinates and
vector quantities consistently and declare the expected transformation of learned features. A ca-
pacity criterion should count the intended physical unit and avoid adding a subset to its parent.
These conditions are illustrated by the retained study; they are not a claim of a universally optimal
acceptance threshold.
7

Several limits remain. The original decoded arrays and model checkpoints were not replayed,
and the pilot’s coordinate and scalar object arrays remain opaque. The complete historical raw
manifest is unresolved at its recorded location. Upstream capacity frames were not independently
reread; exact group classifications and source totals retain their original report status. The one-
case MACE-derived diagnostic is not an independent DFT oracle. Its input-validity check does
not resolve whether a model implementation, training choice or representation causes the observed
output failure. No conservation law is inferred from preservation of copied input identities.
The two inspected primary references establish task context and relevant energy definitions,
rather than a comprehensive novelty survey. This study is a retrospective evaluation case, with
no claim of state-of-the-art performance, physical certification, production readiness or universal
codec failure. Its contribution is the supported separation of otherwise easily conflated evaluation
results.

\section*{6 Conclusion}

The retained adsorbate–slab study shows that small stored-byte reconstruction medians, large tails,
a valid-input symmetry counterexample and an insufficient physical population can coexist. Both
pilot seeds pass their declared median gate, while their maximum saved atom errors approach
19 Å. Complete retained duplicate-group reaggregation reconciles 200079631 distinct frames from
the reported upstream input, below the one-billion target. The historical checkpoint’s saved sym-
metry failure remains distinct from those pilot results, and its input transform is independently
reproduced.
These findings favor reporting each qualification on its own evidence and counting unit. They
preserve the pilot’s demonstrated scope while making its tails, source-capacity limit and outstanding
physical checks visible. Independent prediction replay and matched physical references are the next
requirements for stronger qualification.
