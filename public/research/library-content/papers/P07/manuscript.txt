Explicit coordinate carriers, learned distance tails, and runtime
frame augmentation in an atomwise molecular representation
Abstract
Molecular tokens can contain both explicitly encoded coordinates and learned features, but
these information paths support diﬀerent scientiﬁc claims. We examine a stored atomwise
representation comprising 32 explicit carrier words and 160 learned scalar features per atom,
all stored in BF16 precision. In a 2,048-structure support-balanced diagnostic, the learned
pair-distance decoder had a median structure mean absolute error of 0.05544 angstrom, yet its
global pair-weighted mean was 0.25051 angstrom and its maximum structure mean absolute error
was 2.96539 angstrom. The median satisﬁed the original criterion while the global mean and
maximum failed; the top 100 structures contributed approximately 75% of aggregate absolute
pair error. A separate 4,096-row stored-bank audit reported a carrier componentwise RMS error
median of 2.55 × 10−5 angstrom, which does not validate learned distance prediction. Stored
geometry also exhibited orientation bias: 7.5714% of principal axes in a 32,768-row block sample
lay within 30 degrees of either laboratory z pole, compared with the uniform-axis expectation of
13.3975%. In a diﬀerent, paired loader sample, runtime random-frame treatment changed this
fraction from 7.7484% to 13.7177%, while preserving learned features and limiting the maximum
distance change across 67,763,506 compared pairs to 0.001556 angstrom. The case study shows
why coordinate ﬁdelity, learned-feature utility, error denominators, and orientation diagnostics
must be evaluated separately. It establishes neither a new force ﬁeld nor improved downstream
physical accuracy.
1 Introduction and contributions
A molecular representation may preserve geometry for more than one reason. Coordinates can
be carried explicitly in a compact numerical format, while an encoder separately learns atomwise
features from species and interatomic distances. Accurate decoding from the ﬁrst path does not
demonstrate that the second has learned an equally accurate geometric representation. Likewise,
rotation-invariant learned features do not imply that the coordinate-bearing part of a stored token
has an isotropic orientation distribution.
This paper studies these distinctions in a particular atomwise molecular token containing 192
bﬂoat16 (BF16) words per atom. Its explicit carrier occupies 32 words and its learned scalar lane
occupies 160 words. The scientiﬁc question is not whether this architecture is universally preferable,
but what its saved validation results establish when information paths, metric denominators, and
sampling designs are made explicit.
The contribution has three parts. First, we separate centered-coordinate recovery from learned
pair-distance prediction. Second, we show how a low median structure error can coexist with a
failed global pair-weighted mean and a substantial error tail. Third, we diagnose stored orientation
bias and interpret a runtime random-frame treatment that changes the coordinate carrier without

modifying learned features. These parts are linked by a common principle: validation must identify
both the information being tested and the population or denominator over which it is summarized.
The evidence supports a focused retrospective case study rather than a claim of architectural
novelty or state-of-the-art molecular prediction. The study concerns an atomwise 32+160 represen-
tation, learned distance tails, and runtime orientation treatment, rather than pocket-conditioned
frame recovery for ﬁxed-order coarse-grained windows. Periodic crystal representations are outside
its scope.
2 Related work
Distance-dependent molecular learning has established precedents. SchNet introduced continuous-
ﬁlter convolutions for molecular representations and energy and force prediction [ 1]. It provides
context for learning from continuous molecular geometry, but is not a matched benchmark for the
present encoder or its distance decoder.
The source-family names in the saved records also require careful interpretation. OMol25 de-
scribes molecular DFT data spanning 83 elements [ 2], while Transition1x describes reaction-path
conﬁgurations for reactive molecular learning [ 3]. Here, labels such as OMol retained, OMol expan-
sion, SPICE/MD22, and Transition1x/ANI identify retained source families. They do not establish
that complete published datasets, their preparation procedures, or their benchmark evaluations
were reproduced.
Runtime rotations use the quaternion construction associated with Shoemake’s Uniform Random
Rotations [ 4]. The available supporting material is the C supplement, particularly its quaternion-
generation and matrix-conversion routines, together with the book-series attribution. The captured
loader implements a deterministic, ﬁnite pseudorandom counterpart of that construction. Its empir-
ical validation should therefore be distinguished from a mathematical assertion of exact independent
Haar sampling.
3 Scientiﬁc methods and validation protocols
3.1 Retrospective evidence and validation scope
The present work reanalyzes captured reports, source implementations, and saved validation con-
tracts. Two independent checking recipes contain 166 and 200 checks, respectively, for a total of 366
source/report identity, aggregate-arithmetic, report-linkage, and saved-contract checks. All were
reported as passing. No additional molecular simulations, original checkpoint execution, full-bank
scan, or recovery of all original pair-distance arrays was performed. Thus, recomputable aggregate
arithmetic is distinguished from reported measurements such as medians that require the original
arrays. The captures and checking recipes are preserved in Supplementary record S1.
The principal cohorts have diﬀerent purposes: a 2,048-structure support-balanced diagnostic; an
8,192-structure ﬁxed-test-style reference; a 256-structure informativeness audit; a 4,096-row equal-
family stored-bank audit; and two distinct 32,768-row orientation samples. They are not pooled
into a common held-out population.

3.3 Learned scalar features and pair decoder
The inspected encoder uses atomic-number embeddings, branch embeddings, active/defect/adsorbate
scalar masks, and interatomic radial distances. Its default hidden width is 384, with six scalar
message-passing layers and 32 Gaussian radial basis functions whose centers span 0–16 angstrom.
A normalized linear head followed by Tanh produces 160 learned features per atom. These features
are subsequently stored in BF16.

3.5 Stored-bank and informativeness protocols
The stored-bank reconstruction audit sampled 4,096 rows, with 1,024 rows from each of four source-
labelled families. Selection used 16 manifest-byte blocks per family and 64 contiguous rows per
block. This equal-family design is neither population weighting of the bank nor an independent
held-out sample; reported worst rows include training and validation entries. Supported-element
macro summaries require at least 24 sampled atoms per species.
The saved informativeness audit compared the trained decoder on full learned features, zeroed
features, and features shuﬄed among atoms within each structure. It used 256 structures. Linear
probes used 2,048 training structures and 512 test structures, containing 136,939 and 44,007 atoms.
The species probe used training-set feature standardization and 500 optimization steps. The com-
position ridge probe used mean-pooled features and regularization 10−3 to predict source-labelled
atomic-number fractions. These are reported prior audit executions, not newly executed ablations
in this study.

3.6 Orientation diagnostic and runtime treatment
For centered decoded geometry, the orientation diagnostic forms the geometric covariance matrix
and takes its largest-eigenvalue eigenvector v as an unoriented axis. With eigenvalues λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ λ3,
near-degeneracy is assessed using
δ = (λ1 − λ2)/ max(λ1, 10−30), (13)
with a threshold of 0.001. Near- z alignment means |v · ez| ≥ cos 30◦. A uniform unoriented axis
has double-cap probability
p0 = 1 − cos 30◦ = 0.1339745962. (14)
The saved practical criterion is an absolute deviation from p0 no greater than 0.03, or three per-
centage points.
Both orientation audits sample 128 manifest-byte blocks with 256 contiguous rows per block.
Their seeds and rows diﬀer. The loader audit alone provides paired before/after measurements.
Byte-block selection does not produce independent, identically distributed rows, so we use descrip-
tive eﬀect sizes rather than binomial signiﬁcance calculations.

4 Experimental results
4.1 A low median coexists with mean and maximum failures
Table 1 compares the statistics within each cohort. The 2,048-structure diagnostic contains 13,028,372
ordered pairs. Its median structure MAE of 0.0554395583 angstrom passes the original 0.1884763333
angstrom criterion, but the global pair-weighted mean of 0.2505095165 angstrom and maximum
structure MAE of 2.9653882451 angstrom fail their respective criteria. Six structures exceeded the
original maximum criterion.
The top 100 structures by absolute pair-error contribution account for 0.7499805231 of total
absolute pair error, approximately 75%. Large structures receive more pair weight, and their errors
can therefore dominate the pooled statistic even when most structures have small errors. The
129–256-atom bin contains only 260 of the 2,048 structures but 9,958,994 of its ordered pairs, with
a pair-weighted mean of 0.29632318 angstrom. This identiﬁes a size-associated concentration of
aggregate error without establishing size as its sole cause.
The 2.96538825 angstrom value is not the largest individual pair error. The structure with that
MAE has a reported individual-pair maximum of 16.40097427 angstrom, and another captured row
has an individual-pair maximum of 18.08081627 angstrom. Structure-level MAE and individual-
pair extremes must not be interchanged.

4.2 Learned-feature utility is distinct from carrier precision
The 256-structure informativeness audit reported median distance errors of 0.0541321720, 5.1845675142,
and 2.7935853372 angstrom for full, zeroed, and atom-shuﬄed learned features. The trained de-
coder therefore uses useful information in the learned lane on that audit. These controls do not
quantify all information in the features, establish downstream transfer, or independently validate
an energy model.
The linear species probe achieved micro accuracy 0.99609153 but macro recall 0.68834896. Its
majority-class baseline accuracy of 0.50962347 is a micro baseline, not a macro baseline. High overall
accuracy is thus compatible with substantially weaker average recall across species. Because atomic
number is an encoder input, this is a test of accessible species information rather than discovery of
chemistry. Composition ridge MAE was 0.00608407, below the train-mean baseline of 0.00707591,
for source-labelled atomic-number fraction targets. Both MAEs average over all 83 element fractions
per test structure, including absent elements.
In the separate stored-bank audit, componentwise carrier RMS error had median 2.5504105993 ×
10−5 and maximum 1.6227229159 × 10−4 angstrom. Learned distance errors were much larger: me-
dian structure MAE 0.0451902528, global pair mean 0.1187249969, equal-structure mean 0.0645544105,
and maximum structure MAE 2.4172211842 angstrom. These quantities measure diﬀerent paths
and diﬀerent losses; their contrast is not an accuracy ratio between interchangeable reconstruction
methods.
The saved stored-bank results satisﬁed their inherited statistic-matched criteria, including the
0.0019458734 angstrom componentwise carrier-RMS median bound. Only 36 species met the
minimum support of 24 for supported-element summaries. Their reported macro accuracy was

5 Discussion and limitations
5.1 Three validation questions, not one reconstruction score
The explicit carrier answers whether centered source geometry survives numerical storage. The
learned decoder answers whether atomwise scalar features support accurate pair-distance predic-
tion under a particular trained readout. The orientation audit answers how decoded shapes are
distributed relative to a laboratory axis. A favorable answer to one question cannot substitute for
another.
The tail diagnostic is particularly consequential for heterogeneous molecular sizes. Pair weighting
grows approximately quadratically with atom count, whereas the median gives each structure one
position in an ordered distribution. Both can be useful, but they describe diﬀerent operational
risks. A median summarizes typical structure-level behavior; a pooled pair mean reﬂects the total
absolute error per evaluated pair; and a maximum exposes severe row-level failure. Reporting
7

all three retains information that a single headline error loses. Pair counts are not independent
statistical replicates because pairs share atoms and structures.
Correcting the reference denominator is scientiﬁcally appropriate when it makes the comparison
well deﬁned. It is not evidence that the representation improved. The later contract establishes
consistency relative to the same checkpoint’s ﬁxed-test statistics and stated tolerances. It does
not reverse the original failure or establish application-speciﬁc adequacy. Similarly, passing the
stored-bank audit under inherited bounds does not erase its sampled maximum structure MAE
above 2 angstrom.
5.2 What runtime frames do and do not change
Distance/scalar inputs can yield rotation-invariant learned features even when stored coordinate
carriers retain biased source orientations. An isotropic side-length rule removes directional depen-
dence from the scale, not from the coordinates themselves. Runtime frame augmentation acts on
that remaining pose information.
The paired loader result supports the narrow operational conclusion that ephemeral rotation and
repacking reduced the measured near- z discrepancy while leaving learned features unchanged and
preserving distances within the observed tolerance. It does not repair the stored bank, improve
the encoder, or demonstrate improved training. Moreover, one cap statistic does not characterize
every aspect of an orientation distribution. The ﬁnite deterministic hash construction is useful for
reproducible pseudorandom augmentation, but does not create mathematically exact independent
rotations.
5.3 Evidence and generalization limits
Block samples contain contiguous rows, and byte-based selection can reﬂect manifest layout and
record lengths. The equal-family reconstruction sample additionally imposes artiﬁcial family weights.
Neither design supports an unqualiﬁed whole-bank population estimate. We therefore report de-
scriptive measurements and practical criteria without inferential conﬁdence intervals derived from
row, pair, block, or atom counts. No independent molecular trajectories were generated or analyzed
as statistical replicates in this study.
The veriﬁcation work establishes saved-evidence consistency, not a fresh replication of model
behavior. A later source manifest matches the inspected reconstruction, orientation, informative-
ness, and loader implementations, while an earlier informativeness source diﬀers. A vailability of
those sources does not authenticate every historical job’s exact execution environment. Counts and
medians requiring original arrays remain reported measurements.
The available energy probe uses source-native units whose physical conversion was not frozen, so
it supports no physical energy-accuracy claim here. Authoritative total-charge labels were unavail-
able, spin labels lacked multiple training classes, and a conformer-class ontology was not available.
Species accessibility and composition prediction therefore do not establish charge, spin, conformer,
force, or dynamical generalization. The present evidence should not be interpreted as validation of
a molecular potential.

6 Conclusion
This retrospective case study separates explicit numerical geometry from learned molecular features
and shows why that separation matters for validation. The carrier preserves centered coordinates
with small reported componentwise RMS error, while the learned distance decoder retains a sub-
stantial tail that a low median alone would conceal. Statistic-matched reference comparisons clarify
denominators but do not change predictions or repair the original failed criteria.
Stored orientation is a third, independent property. A block sample revealed a practically impor-
tant principal-axis imbalance, and a distinct paired loader audit showed that runtime random-frame
treatment brought the measured cap fraction close to its uniform-axis target with bounded observed
quantization error. Because the treatment preserves learned features, it is geometry augmentation
rather than representation learning. Together, the results favor explicit reporting of information
paths, weighting rules, tail summaries, and sampling designs over a single undiﬀerentiated recon-
struction claim.
