What Passing Solver Checks Establish: Periodic Decay, Manufactured Pressure, and Failed Boundary Response in Fluid Verification

Working paper · 10 pages

Read PDFDownload PDFDownload LaTeX package

Abstract

A numerical acceptance result applies to an observable, a configuration and a stated toler- ance. We examine the consequences of keeping those conditions explicit across four retained fluid-verification campaigns. Ten periodic Taylor–Green cases passed their recorded limits; stationary decay-energy errors gave observed resolution orders of 1.987–2.054, and a matched moving-frame comparison differed by 0.0868% in its energy-retention ratio. A bounded residual- refresh candidate was admitted on four manufactured pressure graphs, although the original comparison panel retained failed baseline outcomes. In annular Couette startup, independently reconstructed observables from 721 saved planar states agreed with the original reductions, while the physical response still failed: maximum surface-torque errors were 5.53–7.06% of the steady torque scale, aggregate profile error was 7.327%, and final profile error was 5.197%, against 5% limits. Separate fixed-flow S-bend records showed nonmonotone pressure refinement and a corrector-induced change comparable to the medium-to-fine change despite very small surface- flux imbalance. These are configuration-specific tests, not a controlled isolation of one failure mechanism. Their combined contribution is an empirical account of why periodic accuracy, algebraic admission, observable reconstruction and physical boundary response require separate decisions.
